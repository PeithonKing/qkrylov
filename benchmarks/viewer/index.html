SP and TP: two different algorithms

SP = Single-Pass Lanczos (qk.solvers.Lanczos) and TP = Two-Pass Lanczos (qk.solvers.LanczosTwoPass). Both find the same ground state and the same energy; they differ in how they get the eigenvector.

  • Single-pass runs the Lanczos recursion once and keeps every Krylov vector in memory, then builds the eigenvector from them. It is the faster one, but memory grows with the number of iterations: iterations × one state vector.
  • Two-pass keeps only a few vectors. It runs the recursion once to get the energy and the coefficients, then runs it a second time to rebuild the eigenvector without having stored anything. That is twice the matrix–vector products, so it is roughly 2× slower (about 1.2–2× in the measurements above), but it needs a small fraction of the RAM, so much larger systems become reachable at the price of time.

Example: on the 24 GB GPU at L = 28 in FP64, single-pass would need about 30 GB for its 100 stored vectors and runs out of memory (shown as “out of memory”), while two-pass finishes in 3.6 s. FP32 / FP64 are single / double precision.
